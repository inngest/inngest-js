Experimental agents: a channel's progress stream can be started and ended with content, says when it was continued after a restart, and report steps can have stable IDs. `report.begin({ title, recipient, cards })` starts a stream with a title (Slack: the plan title), the person it is for and its first cards, and a stream that rolls over keeps them. `report.finish({ text, blocks })` ends the stream with markdown text and surface-specific blocks (Slack: `chat.stopStream` `markdown_text` and `blocks`, such as feedback buttons); with no open stream it posts the text. A stream's `start` now receives those options and writes the first cards itself, and its `stop` receives the final content. A stream that goes on after the run's `restarts` grew gets one card from the channel's `continued(restarts)` (Slack: "Continued in a new process"), counted against the stream's limits. `report.card()`, `report.status()`, `report.begin()` and `report.finish()` take a `stepId` base for callers whose traces assert one step ID per label; the default IDs are unchanged.

Reports can also ride on your own step. Called inside a running `step.run`, `report.status()`, `report.card()`, `report.begin()` and `report.finish()` write directly instead of making a step of their own, so an app that already writes to Slack inside its steps adds no step and nests none; your step's memo covers the replay. A stream's state is then kept in the thread's durable state (KV calls inside a running step now ride on it too, instead of being nested steps, so an `update`, `take` or `setIfAbsent` there runs again if your step is retried and must be safe to repeat). A write that lands but whose state is not saved before your step fails is written again on the retry, as every write here is at least once. Use one form for a stream: a stream written in one form and then the other starts again.
