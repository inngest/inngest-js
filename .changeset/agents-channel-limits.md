Experimental agents: `report.card()` shows task cards in a channel's progress stream, and the channel owns the surface's limits, so no agent has to. A channel declares a `stream` with its `limits` (Slack: 50 cards, roll over at 40, a stream replaced after nine minutes). When a stream is about to hold more unique cards than allowed, has lived as long as the surface lets it, or the surface says it ended (`message_not_in_streaming_state` on Slack), the reporter starts a new stream whose first card is a summary of the cards it left, and writes only the card at hand, not the history. Writes to a stream run one after another in call order, so parallel tools cannot race it, and each is a step that saves where the stream stands, so a replay writes nothing twice. The Slack channel treats any warning Slack adds to a streamed update (Slack answers `ok` while dropping part of it) as an error. A channel without a `stream` ignores cards. Write your own surface with `defineChannel({ post, stream: { limits, start, append } })`; `append` throws `ChannelStreamEnded` when the stream is over.
